Signed and numbered

Every doll has a name and a number

I create one-of-a-kind folk art dolls from cloth and clay. Each one is designed and made entirely by me, from the first idea to the finished piece, clothing and accessories included. I design, sew, stuff, hand-stitch, paint, photograph and package every piece.

My cloth dolls come signed, named, numbered and dated, with a Certificate of Authenticity. Even when I make several dolls on a similar theme, each one is unique and will never be reproduced.

“They look like wood!”

Muslin, paint and patience

I hear it often: what are these made of? Many of my cloth dolls look carved, but they are made from high quality muslin. The wooden look comes from layers and layers of paint, with light sanding between each one. A light touch reveals a softer, more supple texture, much like worn, broken-in leather.

Inspiration comes from nature, the changing seasons, memories of growing up in Appalachia, vintage postcards, antiques and the stories I read as a child. I reuse what I can, with the make-do attitude this area taught me: vintage buttons, torsos stuffed with rags the way dolls were made long ago, local found objects when they suit the work.

Bits of whimsy

Paintings, ornaments and tree toppers

Besides the dolls, the work table turns out hand-painted stuffed ornaments, quilted ornaments, brooches, tree toppers, story boxes and paintings, for Christmas, Halloween, Easter and every day in between.

Christmas in Berea

I was honored to be asked to create a Christmas collection for the Kentucky Artisan Center in Berea. It filled the three main trees in the lobby of the gallery: a whopper of an order, 283 pieces in all.
