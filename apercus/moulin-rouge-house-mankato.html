An architectural treasure hunt worth preserving.

Chris and Kristin see the house as one large piece of artwork. Some of what caught their eye is the kind of craftsmanship that can't be replicated, and it's all still here for guests to enjoy.

Carved fireplace and stained glass
Why "Moulin Rouge"?

From 1940 to 2015, Betty Webb, then her daughter Cecilia Hanson, ran the Moulin Rouge Beauty Shop in the parlor of the home.

    Inside the turret, with a curved railing and a large round window of multicolored stained-glass triangles.

  • Inlaid wood floors

The house that Mankato kept.

Restoring the home took 3 years, with much of the work done by the owners themselves and the support of local community members, completed "piece by piece."

"It's not just about where you stay, but more importantly, it's about what you do and your experience in a place."
  1. 1886

    The Hunt House

    Built for Lewis P. and Lisbeth Hunt. Lewis owned and edited The Free Press newspaper. The Hunts added the turret with its spiral staircase, the front entry and the parlor with its bay window.

  2. 1938

    The Moulin Rouge Beauty Shop

    Henry and Betty Webb rented, then bought the house. From 1940, for 75 years, it stayed in the Webb-Hanson family, with Betty's beauty shop in the parlor.

  3. 2015

    Almost torn down

    Developers bought the home to build apartments. The City of Mankato said no, because of its historical significance, and the house sat empty.

  4. 2018

    Chris and Kristin step in

    Chris Crowell bought the house, and with Kristin Fisher restored it to its original Queen Anne style before opening the bed and breakfast.

Four rooms, each named for a piece of the story.
