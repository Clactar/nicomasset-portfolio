Currently, we are involved with two separate and highly specialized techniques focusing on “Neo-Primitive” firing.

Our Cosmic Clay journey began nearly 40 years ago, as we collaborated in the creation of art clay vessels. Over the years we have taken our audience down visual avenues of classical, abstract, Deco, and futuristic design.

Two fires

We create two bodies of neo-primitive clayworks: straw-fired by Christine, sawdust-fired by Louis.

Straw-firedby Christine Colombarini

“My clay vessels and sculptural forms are hand-built, slip-cast, or wheel-thrown, sometimes a combination. I often alter the clay by incising, relief-carving, or adding clay shapes.

The bisqued piece is primitive-fired on an open-air grate buried in straw. The results are dramatic: rich sepia tones blending straw patterns and smoky fire clouds. That surface becomes my canvas, and I hand-color each piece with oil sticks, acrylics, and modeling paste.”

Sawdust-firedby Louis Colombarini

“After firing Raku vessels for 25 years, I began to experiment and developed my own primitive sawdust-firing technique. Without gas heat or glaze, each piece is coated with a copper patina and buried in sawdust inside a ventilated metal chamber.

The sawdust burns for three to eight hours. The result is a multitude of spontaneous colors and patterns, impossible to duplicate. I seal the surface with clear acrylic to protect it.”

Caring for a vessel: our pieces will not hold food or water; vase forms can be used with a liner inside. Clean with a soft, lint-free cloth, and never use harsh chemicals.

About us

We met in the clay studio at Florida Atlantic University in Boca Raton, where Christine was earning her B.F.A. in Ceramics.

Christine was born in Manitowoc, Wisconsin, and always knew she'd become an artist. Louis was born in Queens, New York, graduated from the State University of New York at Geneseo, and was a firefighter in Fort Lauderdale for five years while he continued his ceramics studies at FAU.

Our shared love of clay and country living brought us to the hills of Tennessee. For 40 years we have shared a home and a studio, and our work has taken us to over 500 juried art shows and exhibitions, with numerous awards along the way.
