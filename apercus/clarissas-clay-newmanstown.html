Stoneware and porcelain, painted with a bamboo brush.

Functional wheel-thrown pottery, decorated with natural subjects or calligraphy in earth oxides before firing.

See the pottery Visit by appointment
“I use sumi-e style brush painting with bamboo brushes. Its simplicity and relaxed feeling enhance those motifs.”

Clear glazes let my clay bodies show off their natural state, complementing the natural subjects: pine needles, bamboo leaves, birds, a rooster mid-strut.

Handcrafted in the truest sense.

From the clay body to the glaze, I make it myself, then throw, paint and fire each piece.

Made for every day. My glazes are lead-free and durable. The pieces are safe in the dishwasher, the microwave, and an oven that has not been preheated first. Extreme temperature changes could harm them.
  1. 1

    My own clay bodies

    I mix natural, crushed clays and stone into unique clay bodies, and formulate my own slips, stains and glazes.

  2. 2

    Aged before the wheel

    The clay rests six months to a year before it is ready for the potter's wheel.

  3. 3

    Painted with earth oxides

    Natural subjects or calligraphy, brushed on before firing, sumi-e style.

  4. 4

    Two firings, thirty hours

    Each piece spends thirty hours or more in the kiln, through two separate firings at temperatures as high as 2330°F.

Hi, I'm Clarissa.

It began with mud pies and small pots after rainstorms, as a child in Berks County, Pennsylvania. A pottery class through the Denver Free University in 1972 rekindled that passion.

I went on to earn a Bachelor of Arts degree from Millersville University in 1975, then worked as an apprentice potter at the Capricorner near Carlisle, Pennsylvania, in 1976 and 1977.

  • Juried member, Pennsylvania Guild of Craftsmen
  • Juried member, Lancaster Designer Craftsmen
  • Member, Central Pennsylvania Potters Information Group
