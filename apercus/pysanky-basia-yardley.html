Written, not painted.

A pysanka is a Ukrainian Easter egg, decorated with a wax-resist method that dates back to ancient times. The design is "written" with a stylus filled with hot beeswax.

  1. Start with a white egg

    Wax goes on with the stylus. Everything covered now will stay white.

  2. Dip in the first color

    Yellow, for example. New wax lines added now will be yellow in the end.

  3. Layer the colors

    A pysanka can carry anywhere from one to 5 or 6 colors, dipped one after the other.

  4. Melt off the wax

    The egg is warmed over a flame and the wax wiped away, revealing the pattern.

Legend of the pysankaA creature is chained to a hillside. The more pysanky written each year, the tighter its bonds.

If many eggs are decorated, its evil is kept in check for yet another year. Helping to save the world, one pysanka at a time.

Hi, I'm Basia.

I live in Yardley, in southeastern Pennsylvania. Growing up in a Ukrainian family, I learned to write pysanky at an early age and fell in love with it, writing eggs every Lenten season through my school years. Raising a family put the art on a back burner. With my boys grown, I finally brought out my eggs and dyes, and now they stay out all year.

In 2014 I attended my first PysankyUSA Retreat, hosted by Jim Hollock. A class in embellished pendants, taught by Marianne Lurie, started me on eggshell jewelry. One of my great joys is hosting classes and workshops.

Since March 1, 2022, proceeds from purchases on my website go to humanitarian aid in Ukraine, currently through Razom for Ukraine. Slava Ukraini!
