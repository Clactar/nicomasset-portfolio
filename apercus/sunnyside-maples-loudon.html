From Bumfagon Road to Route 106.

In 1955, Lauris Moore and family moved from a sugarhouse on Bumfagon Road in Loudon to a newly built sugarhouse, complete with electric lights and a concrete floor, on busy Route 106. Sunnyside Maples is still operated on that same site.

  1. 1955

    Lauris Moore and family move into the new sugarhouse on Route 106.

  2. 1972

    Sunnyside Maples is formed.

  3. 1981

    Richard and Elaine Moore open a pancake restaurant. The family served on weekends for 23 years, a very popular stop during the season.

  4. Today

    Mike, Cheryl, Nick and Ryan carry on. All of the sap is gathered with tubing and boiled on a high efficiency 3x10 oil-fired evaporator, with a steam-away and reverse osmosis.

Tapping trees, collecting sap, boiling and bottling it with care, producing one of New England’s purest pleasures.

A brief sugaring season

Just south of the Lakes Region, our season begins in late February, when the warm days of approaching spring offset the chill of late winter nights. For the next few weeks, work goes on at a feverish pace, until warmer nights signal the end of another season.

Through the season, our gift shop features all types of maple products made by the Moore family, along with other New Hampshire made items.
